## What information would actually be added?

Let ε be the noise of **unique** source and target image pixels, with covariance Λ. A frozen local photometric linearization has residual

`d_i = J_i δθ_i + S_i ε`,

where S_i includes pixel selection, interpolation, and signs. Its weighted least-squares sensitivity is

`L_i = −(J_iᵀ W_i J_i)⁻¹ J_iᵀ W_i S_i`,

with an additional coordinate-extraction Jacobian absorbed into L_i when needed. Then

`Cov(δz_i, δz_j) = L_i Λ L_jᵀ`.

At a fixed pose, with geometric residual Jacobians T_i, the corresponding blocks are `C_ij = T_i L_i Λ L_jᵀ T_jᵀ`. This is standard linear uncertainty propagation. It contains gradient direction, interpolation weights, and actual shared support that cached coordinates/confidences alone do not provide. Geometric overlap counts are insufficient to determine its sign and magnitude.

This models conditional photometric noise, not automatically DKM's learned error, wrong-basin initialization, occlusion, calibration error, or systematic model bias. Global blur/exposure modes require their own model; they do not follow from independent sensor noise. A single residual vector cannot identify an unrestricted covariance. Any mean improvement from photometric refinement must be held identical across the marginal- and joint-covariance arms.

## Two exact limitations and a compression boundary

**Duplicate-group equivalence.** For m measurements with a common scalar prediction and covariance `σ² I + τ² 11ᵀ`, the GLS estimator uses the group mean with precision `m/(σ²+mτ²)`. This is exactly a weighted group-mean control. In the exact-duplicate limit, dropping duplicate copies preserves information. Gains over counting every copy independently therefore do not establish a need for a sophisticated joint estimator.

**Pose-aligned noise cannot change the local point estimate.** For full-column-rank A, positive-definite D, and `y=Aθ+ε`, let `C=D+AΓAᵀ`, with Γ positive semidefinite. Writing `G=AᵀD⁻¹A`, Woodbury gives

`(AᵀC⁻¹A)⁻¹ AᵀC⁻¹ = G⁻¹ AᵀD⁻¹`.

Thus GLS and D-weighted LS have identical estimates; covariance becomes `G⁻¹+Γ`. This is a local linear-model statement, not a universal claim about nonlinear robust optimization. A noise mode indistinguishable from pose cannot be removed merely by recognizing its covariance.

**Compression is conditional.** For fixed covariance C and algebraic epipolar rows A, `eᵀ Aᵀ C⁻¹ A e` admits a 9×9 summary. For a frozen local pose linearization, `H=JᵀC⁻¹J` and `g=JᵀC⁻¹r` similarly preserve the quadratic step. These are standard weighted reduced-measurement/normal-equation identities. They do not make construction free: residual covariance depends on pose through T, robust weights may change, and sparse shared supports do not imply a sparse inverse. Cross-cluster covariance prevents independent per-cluster summaries unless separators or omitted couplings are controlled.

The potentially useful increment is an inexpensive construction/update of the **image-derived dependence operator**, with an explicit approximation domain, retaining the useful joint-GLS information at lower total cost. It must beat independent affine covariance, density/group controls, and DMS at fixed total time including image work. Neither Making Affine's checked construction nor DMS's checked objective already supplies that operator; this limited observation does not establish novelty. Calling a standard Schur complement or a frozen weighted Gram matrix a new theorem would be unjustified.

For fixed linear Gaussian image formation, compressing unique pixels cannot increase Fisher information. With pixel covariance Λ, image Jacobian B and summary operator L, `Bᵀ Lᵀ(LΛLᵀ)^+ L B ≼ BᵀΛ⁻¹B`. A matched unique-pixel likelihood is therefore an information reference. Full-covariance feature processing can repair double counting, not create observations beyond the images.

## Minimal decisive artificial test, not yet executed

Use a fixed, well-conditioned two-view scene with several textured planar patches and full-rank pose information. Generate each unique image pixel's noise once, so overlapping fits share it. Pair this with independent patch-copy noise having the same marginal distributions and geometry. Freeze fitting, correspondence means, support choices, and noise level across covariance arms.

Compare diagonal per-affine covariance, oracle joint covariance, the analytic shared-pixel construction, group-mean/density or spatial-subsampling controls, and a matched unique-pixel likelihood. Include a shuffled off-diagonal control preserving marginal covariance. Separately verify the exact duplicate and pose-aligned identities above. Evaluate pose error over independent noise realizations as well as predicted versus empirical covariance; treat an oracle as a mechanism ceiling, never a deployable method.

An accuracy mechanism survives only if oracle joint covariance improves translation estimation beyond the cheap group control, the analytic construction reproduces that gain, and the gain responds to the shared-versus-independent intervention. If it only changes confidence, or all benefit is duplication correction, stop the accuracy claim. Compression additionally needs a measured advantage over direct joint GLS at matched accuracy and all setup costs charged. Pose-dependent covariance needs either a declared frozen approximation or the corresponding likelihood normalization/derivatives; arbitrary componentwise Cauchy loss after whitening is not invariant to the whitening basis.

The falsifiable real-world reason for improvement is that shared photometric disturbances have components distinguishable from pose and affect redundant regions differently from independent informative regions. This can reduce translation variance when geometry is observable. It does not predict correction of deterministic matcher bias or recovery of absent parallax. No real-data evidence for these conditions has been established here.

## Proposed first image-information diagnostic — design only

The first pilot should test ordinary image-based localization before implementing joint covariance. This is an explicitly **asymmetric adaptation** of known photometric registration, not a reproduction of the symmetric ECCV 2020 method: the stored first-image coordinate p is a canonical query pixel and remains fixed; the second-image coordinate q may change. DKM's sampling direction must be recorded rather than assuming every stored match was originally queried in this direction.

One proposed fixed configuration, subject to a frozen protocol before image access:

- Use all 16 existing development pairs, preserving every attempted pair and patch, with no new scene or label-driven selection. Select at most 256 source queries by a 16×16 image grid, one highest-confidence stored match per occupied cell (original index breaks ties). Do not fill empty cells or change selection after fitting. This bucketing is a known coverage control, not a contribution.
- Use a 31×31 grayscale source window in raw-image pixels. Normalize intensity to [0,1] with a fixed RGB-to-gray mapping recorded in the protocol. This window is an engineering choice providing 961 samples for eight unknowns at modest cost; it is **not a published default**. No scale or window sweep on the 16 labels.
- Initialize the 2×2 target affine map by ordinary centered local least squares on the 32 nearest other cached source matches, fitting target displacements relative to the selected stored pair. Record neighbor distances and fit conditioning. A finite full-rank initialization is required; otherwise preserve a failed-patch record. This is known coordinate-neighbor affine fitting, not new image evidence. Camera geometry or ground truth must not choose neighbors, windows, or initialization.
- Fit `I2(q + δ + A u) − g I1(p+u) − b` over source offsets u, starting δ=0, g=1, b=0. Use forward-additive affine registration, bilinear interpolation with its piecewise analytic gradient, and ordinary squared photometric error. Bilinear interpolation is a deliberate inexpensive adaptation, not ECCV 2020's bicubic symmetric fit. No covariance or robust-loss tuning is part of this pilot.
- Reserve the fixed offset pattern `(u_x + 2u_y) mod 4 = 0` for photometric validation; fit the remaining offsets. Validation error is a diagnostic, not a patch-selection threshold. Neighboring interpolated samples can share pixels, so this is not an independent statistical test.
- A concrete optimizer proposal is 30 LM attempts including rejected steps, initial damping 10^-3 times the normal diagonal, accepted only on strict training-cost decrease, damping divided/multiplied by 10 on acceptance/rejection, and an absolute diagonal floor 10^-12. Normalize affine columns using offsets divided by 15 during the solve. Declare convergence from small predicted decrease or step before running; those tolerances and any conditioning cutoff are engineering parameters that still need synthetic calibration and prospective freezing. Do not infer convergence merely from reaching the attempt limit.

The final bullet is deliberately not an executable protocol: synthetic checks must fix its convergence and conditioning rules once, without the real labels. Border violations, collapsed normal systems, nonfinite arithmetic, failed initialization, and nonconvergence remain visible failure categories. A patch crossing depth boundaries or violating brightness constancy can have a numerically valid but geometrically wrong update; low training loss does not establish accurate localization. Report holdout error change, update magnitude, affine conditioning, and acceptance coverage without using them retrospectively to filter pose results.

**Matched controls.** Freeze one geometric estimator/settings/starting rule for all arms. Compare (a) all selected original matches, (b) original coordinates of exactly the patches classified as successful by the prospectively fixed technical rules, and (c) the same points as (b), with only target coordinates updated. The decisive new-information comparison is c versus b; a versus b measures selection effects. Keep the full 10,000-match baseline as a separate required reference. Also run the full 10,000 set with successful selected target coordinates replaced, retaining every other original match; this tests whether image updates improve the strong existing input rather than only a sparse subset. These are fixed comparisons, not options to select after seeing labels. All pairs count; insufficient-point and resource failures need explicit coverage and predeclared all-pair treatment.

Photometric loss/validation changes can establish that the images provide a signal beyond the stored coordinates, but cannot establish pose improvement by themselves. Seal all output coordinates and failure records before geometric ground-truth evaluation. The 16 previously inspected development labels cannot serve as independent confirmation; a positive result here would only justify a later untouched scene split.

**Batch feasibility.** With B=64 queries, a 31×31 patch produces about 61,500 sampled values per batch. Eight float64 Jacobian channels occupy about 3.9 MB; gather indices, intensities, masks, and temporaries fit comfortably below the 8 GiB cap. Vectorize interpolation and residual/Jacobian formation, accumulate 8×8 systems with `einsum(..., optimize=False)`, and solve small per-patch systems. This avoids the previously observed large-matrix Accelerate floating-point flag problem while retaining finite checks. At 256 queries, 16 pairs, and 30 attempts, the declared ceiling is about 118 million training-plus-validation sample positions before interpolation bookkeeping; this is a feasibility estimate, not a timing result. Decode one image pair at a time, impose a 30-minute total technical budget, retain completed and timed-out cases, and do not retry with easier parameters.

**Future sensitivity hook.** At the final fit, the eight Jacobian columns are target gradients times the translation/affine coordinate bases, followed by `−I1` and `−1` for gain and bias. Preserve original pixel indices and interpolation weights, not only sampled intensity arrays. For each residual, S contains target interpolation weights and source weights multiplied by −g. The first two rows of `−(JᵀJ)⁻¹ Jᵀ S` then give the conditional target-location sensitivity to unique image pixels. It describes the frozen local photometric solution; unknown sensor covariance, noisy gradients, initializer dependence, wrong basins, and model mismatch remain separate limitations. No cross-covariance estimator is admitted by this pilot design.
