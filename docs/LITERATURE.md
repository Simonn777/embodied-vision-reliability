## Consequences for the first protocol

These are design decisions inferred from the literature, not results established by it.

- **Keep the estimator fixed.** Compare rejection scores on identical coordinates. Improving the tracker and improving rejection are different experiments. Diagnostic 0001 compares current-image confidence, causal temporal consistency, random ranking, acceptance of every available estimate, and a clearly labeled error oracle; it has no learned model. A calibration-fitted error predictor and interval-width rejection are proposed stronger controls for a later protocol, once enough independent calibration sequences exist.
- **Define the regression loss.** Use a preregistered localization error in a stated coordinate system and, separately, a fixed large-error threshold. Classification AURC with binary mistakes and regression AURC with pixel error have different meanings and scales. A confidence score need not be a calibrated probability.
- **Separate two meanings of coverage.** Selective coverage is the fraction of predictions accepted. Conformal coverage is the fraction of targets lying inside prediction sets. Neither implies the other. A marginal interval guarantee does not by itself bound error among accepted predictions, especially under shift.
- **Enforce causality.** At time t, the estimator and rejection score may use only allowed information through t. Exclude future frames, future ground-truth visibility, hindsight track smoothing, and revised past predictions from a zero-lookahead result. Report any window delay explicitly.
- **Challenge smoothness.** A smoothly drifting wrong track can look consistent, while a correct track after abrupt motion can look inconsistent. Include both failure modes, occlusion/reappearance, missing outputs, and appearance-confusable distractors. Do not tune these controls on final-test labels.
- **Preserve the experimental unit.** Partition and resample whole sequences/scenes, pair methods on identical examples, specify tie handling, and show accepted error together with actual acceptance. A threshold chosen on calibration can yield a different acceptance rate under shift.

The immediate deliverable is a diagnostic evaluation, not a novel tracking method. Novelty remains unresolved until the closest tracking-confidence, motion-consistency, selective-regression, and sequential-calibration literature is read in depth and compared experimentally.
