## 1. Privileged targets may not be predictable from the causal head inputs

The teacher can choose 128 coefficients separately for each of 48 anchors using its two future features and TRAIN masks. The student is one 33,408-parameter MLP receiving only 64 channel means, 64 standard deviations, presence, predicted IoU, and mask area. It does not receive spatial arrangement or explicit motion. Two memories with the same channel statistics and scalar cues can require different coefficients because their spatial relation to a future query differs. Squared regression tends toward a conditional mean target; conflicting target directions can cancel. Four TRAIN parents also make low training error weak evidence of cross-parent generalization. These are concrete capacity/information/generalization risks, not proof that the chosen features are insufficient on this dataset.

**Saved-result checks, once complete:**

- From all 48 teacher rows, report per-parent and equal-parent `baseline_loss - selected_loss`, positive-gain fraction, selected step, selected-state BF16 changed entries and post-cast RMS. Separately derive the one-step gain from `losses[one_step]`. Retain all anchors and zeros. Because state zero is a candidate, `selected_loss <= baseline_loss` is guaranteed by selection; only the gain's magnitude and its realized storage change provide evidence of a useful training target.
- From saved `targets.pt`, use the recorded Gram and radius to report the normalized zero-predictor error `q_targetᵀGq_target / B²` for each arm (zero when B is zero), and target energy/direction variation by parent. Existing regression losses are already in this metric. Low teacher loss alone says nothing about this target's causal predictability.
- Use the complete six-epoch regression curves and fixed final checkpoints. The logged per-update losses are measured at changing, pre-update heads and shuffled anchors; their epoch mean is **not** a fixed final-head training score. If a stronger fit-versus-generalization diagnosis is needed later, a declared saved-checkpoint/saved-target pass through only the tiny head can measure final per-parent error without SAM2 or new labels. That pass is not executed or silently assumed here.
- Interpret controls conditionally: poor final target fit suggests optimization or inconsistent targets; good target fit but poor DEV performance leaves parent generalization and rollout mismatch. Direct same-head success with KD failure would specifically weaken the usefulness of the regression target/metric. GT-memory KD matching multistep KD would weaken the claimed value of future optimization. These comparisons do not identify a unique cause by themselves.

## 2. Fixed-context surrogate improvement may not survive causal repeated writes

Teacher/direct training replaces **one** memory while all other baseline memories and future image features remain fixed, at horizons +1 and +3. Deployment changes every noninitial write and regenerates all subsequent memories and pointers. Its inputs therefore drift from the baseline contexts used for training. The optimized loss is selected raw pre-presence-mask BCE plus presence BCE at 256², whereas reported J/F thresholds the native output at original resolution; the argmax candidate and BF16 storage are additional nonsmooth operations. A bounded pre-cast residual is neither a bound on accumulated rollout perturbation nor a guarantee of a boundary-F improvement.

**Saved-result checks, once complete:**

- Join all rollout `writes` (`changed_bf16_entries`, pre/post-BF16 RMS) with the complete original-resolution per-frame J and F versus native. Report both parents and all frames; plot the full signed trajectory rather than selecting favorable intervals. Frames 0 and 1 precede any corrective write that can affect their emitted predictions and should retain native masks. A task difference can first occur at frame 2. This is a useful causal-placement check, not a new accuracy gate.
- If coefficients or pre-BF16 RMS change but nearly every stored write remains unchanged, call this a quantized no-op limitation. If actual writes change and teacher gain is positive but own-rollout J/F declines, the experiment shows a training-to-task transfer failure. A later-growing decline is consistent with history drift but does not prove it: scene difficulty also changes with time.
- Compare J and F separately. J improvement with F loss is a genuine task tradeoff; do not hide it in the average or infer boundary quality from BCE. Compare multistep, one-step, GT-memory KD, and both direct controls on their complete own rollouts with actual complete costs.
- Current teacher receipts store the combined two-future BCE, not each mask/presence component or the teacher-modified future masks. They cannot separate “presence loss improved,” “native candidate changed,” and “teacher segmentation genuinely improved” from saved scalars alone. Nor does the current record contain a changed-history, fixed-head counterfactual. Preserve this uncertainty rather than attributing failure to one of those mechanisms or launching an unplanned model replay.

## Addendum after parent-reported teacher metadata, before rollout accuracy

The parent reports teacher loss 0.0184591 → 0.0174126, nonzero BF16 changes for every anchor, and online multistep KD loss near 0.551 after six epochs. These figures were supplied in a message, not independently recomputed here. They motivate the fixed-checkpoint diagnostic above, but do not yet establish that the student poorly fits relative to its target's size. One-step and GT-memory targets have different energies; their lower absolute normalized losses are not directly comparable fit-quality evidence.

For a precise saved-target check, evaluate the final frozen head once on all 48 saved TRAIN inputs and compare its measured-Gram error with the zero predictor using identical normalization, per parent. No attention/decoder or new labels are needed. If a constant baseline is desired, define one shared **dimensionless head-output vector** of length 128; each anchor then uses its original `sigma*rho` scaling and projection. A raw average of coefficients is not that same-head baseline. Fitting the constant vector would be a separate small TRAIN-only diagnostic (for example the already specified Adam/epoch/seed convention), not a deployed arm or proof of the optimal constant. It requires a separate recorded run and is not executed by this review.

Failure to beat zero/constant indicates that the current head inputs and training have not captured useful anchor-specific targets. Success on those comparisons establishes training fit, not causal predictability on new parents. Neither result alone warrants “the privileged component is inherently unpredictable.” Any such stronger claim would need separate generalization evidence; the immediate task evidence remains the planned full own-rollout comparison.
