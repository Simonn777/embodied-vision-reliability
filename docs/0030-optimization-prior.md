## Closest primary mechanisms actually read

**LWL — Bhat et al., “Learning What to Learn for Video Object Segmentation,” ECCV 2020.** Read §§3.1–3.3, 3.5–3.6 and the relevant §4.1 ablations. Equation (2) fits target-specific convolution parameters to a learned encoding of the supplied mask. Equation (3) is a weighted, regularized squared encoding loss; (4) implements differentiable steepest descent. Segmentation errors on subsequent training frames train the representation, label generator and weights through the learner. Inference still uses iterative target-model learning and updates with predicted-mask samples. Thus neither a learned target encoding nor shaping its learning with subsequent frames is new. The inspected mechanism is not a causal network trained to reproduce an independently optimized spatial-memory residual while keeping the segmentation model frozen. [Official ECCV full text](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123470766.pdf)

**DiMP — Bhat et al., “Learning Discriminative Model Prediction for Tracking,” ICCV 2019.** Read §§3.2–3.4, 3.6–3.7, Algorithm 1 and Eq. (9). A learned initializer and differentiable optimizer predict a discriminative target filter. The update is derived steepest descent with a Gauss–Newton step length; loss components are learned, rather than the whole update being an unconstrained neural optimizer. Offline training samples model-building frames from the first half of a segment and evaluation frames from its second half. All optimization iterates, including initialization, receive the later-frame classification objective. Optimization also remains in online tracking. This directly precedes the broad “learn to predict useful target parameters using future training frames” rationale. [Author arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.07220), [official ICCV paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Bhat_Learning_Discriminative_Model_Prediction_for_Tracking_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)

## Consequences for the current development comparison

1. **Keep the direct same-head future-loss controls.** Identical causal inputs, residual family, frozen context, TRAIN labels and rollout policy isolate whether constructing and regressing optimized targets helps. Report both update-matched and complete-cost comparisons. Implementing an entire older tracking architecture is unnecessary to answer this prototype question.
2. **Keep one-step and GT-memory targets.** Multistep versus one-step separates accumulated optimization from a gradient-like teaching signal; the projected GT-memory target checks whether ordinary supervised memory correction explains the effect. The proposed candidate already uses ordinary same-teacher KD, so adding another identically defined “plain KD” arm would duplicate it.
3. **Separate three observations:** teacher loss reduction, causal target predictability, and the student's own-rollout J/F. None implies the next. Fixed other memories and future features define fixed-context training, not full future BPTT.
4. **State the metric qualification.** Current targets use measured-Gram spatial error and a Gram-preconditioned teacher step. A local one-step/direct-gradient relationship needs that matched metric, inactive projection and the stated linearization; it is not an unadjusted Euclidean-coefficient theorem. BF16 and native selection remain explicit surrogate-gradient limits.

Continuing the authorized development campaign is methodologically reasonable. Any eventual contribution must be supported by the memory-write formulation, causal generalization and measured task/cost benefit, not by renaming optimization, future supervision or distillation. The independent-source final +3 J&F gate remains unchanged.
